# 4. Current Research — SSRGNet Protein Structure Paper - in writing paper

* Currently helping with the research paper:
  **“Protein Secondary Structure Prediction Using 3D Graphs and Relation-Aware Message Passing Transformers.”**
* The paper proposes **SSRGNet (Secondary Structure Relational Graph Neural Network)** for Protein Secondary Structure Prediction (PSSP).
* Goal: combine **protein sequence information with 3D structural information** rather than relying only on amino-acid sequences.
* Architecture combines:

  * **DistilProtBERT / protein language model** for sequence representations.
  * **GCN / R-GCN** for structural and relational information.
  * Protein-residue graphs where residues are nodes.
* Three graph relations are modeled:

  * **R1 — Sequential Relationship**
  * **R2 — Spatial Proximity**
  * **R3 — Local Environment**. 
* Evaluates different fusion approaches:

  * Serial fusion
  * Parallel fusion
  * Cross fusion
* **Parallel fusion** was selected as the final strategy because it generally performed best across the evaluated datasets. 
* Datasets:

  * NetSurfP-2.0
  * CB513
  * CASP12
  * TS115
* Evaluates both **3-state and 8-state protein secondary structure prediction**.
* Reported 3-state results show SSRGNet improving over DistilProtBert on several evaluation sets. 
* Important limitations identified in the paper:

  * Noisy/incomplete structural data
  * Limited graph-edge definitions
  * Difficulty capturing long-range dependencies
  * Sequence/structure feature redundancy
  * Class imbalance in rare structural states
  * **Batch size of 1 due to computational/memory constraints**.

I am currently working as an ML Engineer at Unity Bank, where my primary project focuses on customer deduplication, entity resolution, and customer identity management. The bank uses Posidex to consolidate customer records and assign a unique customer identifier across different records and banking relationships. Posidex itself describes this type of system as linking and deduplicating customer records across different systems to create a unified customer view.

A key challenge I am investigating is the occurrence of duplicate customer identities, where the same individual can end up being associated with multiple unique customer IDs because of variations or inconsistencies in their underlying information. For example, customer names may appear in different orders, spellings, formats, or transliterations, while identifiers such as PAN and other demographic attributes may contain formatting or data-quality variations. These differences can cause the existing matching logic to treat records belonging to the same individual as separate customers.

My work involves analysing the existing deduplication and matching methodology, identifying cases where the system fails to correctly link records, and building evidence of its limitations. I am particularly focused on discovering groups of records where multiple unique customer IDs appear to correspond to the same individual, investigating the attributes responsible for the missed matches, and understanding where the existing matching rules or similarity mechanisms break down. The broader objective is to assess whether a more modern ML/AI-based entity-resolution approach could improve upon the existing system in terms of matching accuracy, robustness, and coverage.
